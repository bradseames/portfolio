### 9.4.3 Pin Bending Strength for Double Shear Joints Under Uniform Axial Load

Although actual pin bending failures are infrequent, excessive pin deflections can cause the
load in the lugs to peak up near the shear planes instead of being uniformly distributed across
the lug thickness, thereby leading to premature lug or bushing failures at loads less than
those predicted by Equation (9-11). At the same time, however, the concentration of load near
the lug shear planes reduces the bending arm and, therefore, the bending moment in the pin,
making the pin less critical in bending. The following procedure is used in determining the pin
ultimate bending load.

Assume that the load in each lug is uniformly distributed across the lug thickness
$ (\text{b}_1 = \text{t}_1\text{, and } 2 \ \text{b}_2 = \text{t}_2 ) $. For the symmetrical
joint shown in Figure 9-5, the resulting maximum pin bending moment is

$$
\text{M}_{\text{maxP}} = \frac{\text{P}}{2} \left[ \frac{\text{t1}}{2} + \frac{\text{t2}}{4} + g
\right]
$$

The ultimate failing moment for the pin is

$$
\text{M}_{\text{u}_{\text{p}}} = 0.0982 \ \text{k}_{\text{b}_{\text{P}}} \ {{\text{D}}_{\text{P}}}
^3 \ \text{F}_{\text{tu}_{\text{p}}}
$$

where
$ \text{k}_{\text{b}_{\text{P}}} $ is the plastic bending coefficient for the pin. The value of
$ \text{k}_{\text{b}_{\text{P}}} $ varies from 1.0 for a perfectly elastic pin to 1.7
for a perfectly plastic pin, with a value of 1.56 for pins made from reasonably ductile
materials (more than 5% elongation).

The pin ultimate bending load
$ ( \text{P}_{\text{ub}_{\text{p}}} ) $ is, therefore,

$$
\text{P}_{\text{ub}_{\text{p}}} = \frac{0.1963 \ \text{k}_{\text{b}_{\text{P}}} \ {{\text{D}}_{\text
{P}}}^3 \ \text{F}_{\text{tu}_{\text{p}}}}{\left[ \frac{\text{t1}}{2} + \frac{\text{t2}}{4} + g
\right]} \quad \text{(9-15)}
$$


If
$ \text{P}_{\text{ub}_{\text{p}}} $ is equal to or greater than either
$ \text{P}_{{\text{u}}_{{\text{L}_\text{B}}}} $ (Equation (9-11) or
$ \text{P}_{\text{us}_{\text{p}}} $ (Equation (9-12)), then the pin is a relatively strong pin
that is not critical in bending, and no further pin bending calculations are required. The
allowable load for the joint
$ (\text{P}_{\text{all}}) $ can be determined by going directly to Equation (9-19a).

If
$ \text{P}_{\text{ub}_{\text{p}}} $ (Equation (9-15)) is less than both
$ \text{P}_{{\text{u}}_{{\text{L}_\text{B}}}} $ (Equation (9-11)) and
$ \text{P}_{{\text{us}}_{\text{p}}} $ (Equation (9-12)), the pin is considered a relatively
weak pin, critical in bending. However, such a pin may deflect sufficiently under load to shift
the c.g of the bearing loads toward the shear faces of the lugs, resulting in a decreased pin
bending moment and an increased value of
$ \text{P}_{\text{ub}} $. These shifted loads are assumed to be uniformly distributed over
widths
$ \text{b}_1 \text{ and } 2\text{b}_2 $, which are less than
$ \text{t}_1 \text{ and } \text{t}_2 $, respectively, as shown in Figure 9-5. The portions of
the lugs and bushings not included in
$ \text{b}_1 \text{ and } 2\text{b}_2 $ are considered ineffective. The new increased value of
pin ultimate bending load, is

$$
\text{P}_{\text{ub}_{\text{p}}} = \frac{0.1963 \ \text{k}_{\text{b}_{\text{P}}} \ {{\text{D}}_{\text
{P}}}^3 \ \text{F}_{\text{tu}_{\text{p}}}}{\left[ \frac{\text{b1}}{2} + \frac{\text{b2}}{4} + g
\right]} \quad \text{(9-15a)}
$$

The maximum allowable value of
$ \text{P}_{\text{ub}_{\text{p}}} $ is reached when
$ \text{b}_1\text{ and } \text{b}_2 $ are sufficiently reduced so that
$ \text{P}_{\text{ub}_{\text{p}}} $ (Equation(9-15a)) is equal to
$ \text{P}_{\text{u}_{{\text{L}}_{\text{B}}}} $ (Equation(9-11)),provided that
$ \text{b}_1 \text{ and } \text{2b}_2 $ are substituted for
$ \text{t}_1 \text{and } \text{t}_2 $, respectively. At this point we have a balanced design
where the joint is equally critical in pin-bending failure or lug-bushing failure.

The following equations give the "balanced design" pin ultimate bending load
$ (\text{P}_{\text{ub}_{{\text{p}}_{\text{ max}}}}) $ and effective bearing widths
$ (\text{b}_{1 \text{ min}} \text{and } \text{2b}_{2 \text{ min}}) $ :

$$
\text{P}_{\text{ub}_{\text{p max}}} = 2C \ \sqrt{\frac{\text{P}_{\text{ub}_{\text{p}}}}{C} \ {\left[
\frac{\text{t1}}{2} + \frac{\text{t2}}{4} + g
\right]} + g^2} - 2Cg \quad \text{
(9-16)}
$$

where
$$
C = \frac{\text{P}_{\text{u}_{{\text{L}}_{\text{B1}}}} \ \text{P}_{\text{u}_{{\text{L}}_{\text
{B2}}}}}{\text{P}_{\text{u}_{{\text{L}}_{\text{B1}}}} \ \text{t}_2 + \text{P}_{\text{u}_{{\text
{L}}_{\text{B2}}}} \ \text{t}_1  }
$$

The value of
$ \text{P}_{\text{ub}_{\text{p}}} $ on the right hand side of Equation (9-16) and the values of
$ \text{P}_{\text{u}_{{\text{L}}_{\text{B1}}}} \text{ and } \text{P}_{\text{u}_{{\text{L}}_{\text
{B2}}}} $ in the expression for C are based on the assumption that the full thicknesses of the
lugs are effective and have already been calculated. (Equations (9-10) and (9-15)).

If the inner lug strength is equal to the total strength of the two outer lugs
$ \text{P}_{\text{u}_{{\text{L}}_{\text{B2}}}} = 2\text{P}_{\text{u}_{{\text{L}}_{\text{B1}}}} $
, and if g = 0, then

$$
\text{P}_{\text{ub}_{\text{p max}}} = \sqrt{\text{P}_{\text{ub}_{\text{p}}}  \ \text{P}_{\text{u}_{{\text{L}}_{\text
{B2}}}}} \quad (9-17)
$$

The "balanced design" effective bearing widths are

$$
\text{b}_{\text{1}_{\text{min}}} = \frac{\text{P}_{\text{ub}_{\text{p max}}} \ \text{t}_1}{2 \ \text
{P}_{\text{u}_{{\text{L}}_{\text{B1}}}}} \quad (9-18a)
$$

$$
2\text{b}_{\text{2}_{\text{min}}} = \frac{\text{P}_{\text{ub}_{\text{p max}}} \ \text{t}_2}{
\text{P}_{\text{u}_{{\text{L}}_{\text{B2}}}}} \quad (9-18a)
$$

where
$ \text{P}_{\text{ub}_{\text{p max}}} $ is obtained from Equation (9-16) and
$ \text{P}_{\text{u}_{{\text{L}}_{\text{B1}}}} \text{ and } \text{P}_{\text{u}_{{\text{L}}_{\text{B2}}}} $
are the previously calculated values based on the full thicknesses of the lugs. Since any lug
thicknesses greater than
$ \text{b}_{1 \text{ min}} \text{ or } \text{b}_{2 \text{ min}} $ are not considered effective,
an efficient static strength design would have
$ \text{t}_1 = \text{b}_{1 \text{ min}} $ and
$ \text{t}_2 = \text{b}_{2 \text{ min}} $

The allowable joint ultimate load
$ (\text{P}_{\text{all}}) $ for the double-shear joint is obtained, as follows:

$ \text{P}_{\text{ub}_{{\text{p}}}} $ (Equation (9-15)) is greater than either
$ \text{P}_{\text{u}_{{\text{L}}_{\text{B}}}} $ (Equation (9-11) or
$ \text{P}_{\text{us}_{{\text{p}}}} $ (Equation (9-12)), then
$ \text{P}_{\text{all}} $ is the lower of the values of
$ \text{P}_{\text{u}_{{\text{L}}_{\text{B}}}} $ or
$ \text{P}_{\text{us}_{{\text{p}}}} $

$ \text{P}_{\text{all}} \leq \text{P}_{\text{u}_{{\text{L}}_{\text{B}}}} $ (Equation (9-11) or
$ \text{P}_{\text{us}_{{\text{p}}}} $ (Equation (9-12))

If
$ \text{P}_{\text{ub}_{{\text{p}}}} $ (Equation (9-15)) is less than both
$ \text{P}_{\text{u}_{{\text{L}}_{\text{B}}}} $ and
$ \text{P}_{\text{us}_{{\text{p}}}} $, then
$ \text{P}_{\text{all}} $ is the lower of the values of
$ \text{P}_{\text{us}_{{\text{p}}}} $ and
$ \text{P}_{\text{ub}_{{\text{p max}}}} $.
