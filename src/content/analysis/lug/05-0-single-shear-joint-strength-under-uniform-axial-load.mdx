## 9.5 Single-Shear Joint Strength Under Uniform Axial Load

In single-shear joints, lug and pin bending are more critical than in
double-shear joints. The amount of bending can be significantly affected by
bolt clamping. In the cases considered in Figure 9-6, no bolt clamping is
assumed, and the bending moment in the pin is resisted by socket action in
the lugs.

Figure 9-6. Single Shear Lug Joint

In Figure 9-6 a representative single-shear joint is shown, with centrally
applied loads (P) in each lug, and bending moments (M and Ml) that keep
the system in equilibrium. (Assuming that there is no gap between the lugs,
M + Ml = P (t + tl)/Z). The individual values of M and M, are determined
from the loading of the lugs as modified by the deflection, if any, of the lugs,
according to the principles of mechanics.
