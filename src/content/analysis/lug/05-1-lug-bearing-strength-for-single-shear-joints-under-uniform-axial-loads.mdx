### 9.5.1 Lug Bearing Strength for Single Shear Joints Under Uniform Axial Loads

The bearing stress distribution between lug and bushing is assumed to be similar to the stress
distribution that would be obtained in a rectangular cross section of width
$(\text{D})$ and depth
$(\text{t})$, subjected to a load
$(\text{P})$ and moment
$(\text{M})$. At ultimate load the maximum lug bearing stress
$(\text{F}_{\text{br}_{\text{max}_\text{L}}})$ is approximated by

$$
\text{F}_{\text{br}_{\text{max}_\text{L}}} =
\frac{\text{P}}{\text{Dt}} + \frac{6 \ \text{M}}{\text{K}_{\text{br}_{\text{L}}} \ \text{Dt}^2}
\qquad \text{(9-23)}
$$

where
$\text{K}_{\text{br}_{\text{L}}}$ is a plastic bearing coefficient for the lug material, and is
assumed to be the same as the plastic bending coefficient
$(\text{K}_{\text{b}_{\text{L}}})$ for a rectangular section.

The allowable ultimate value of
$\text{F}_{\text{br}_{\text{max}_\text{L}}}$ is either
$\text{F}_{\text{bru}_{\text{L}}}$ (Equations (9-1a) (9-1b)) or
$1.304 \text{F}_{\text{bry}_{\text{L}}}$ (Equations (9-2a) (9-2b)),
whichever is lower.
